Section 01

Executive Summary

This package contains title documents tracing the Templeton family's mineral and surface interests across multiple tracts in Gregg County, Texas, principally on the Haden Edwards Survey, Abstract No. 64. The records span from approximately 1900 through 2023 and include warranty deeds, mineral deeds, oil and gas leases, probate proceedings, heirship affidavits, OPR index searches, and current operator correspondence.

The core tract is roughly 173 acres (reduced to ~119 acres net of exceptions) that passed through the Templeton family via a 1932 Harrison County partition, was later conveyed by Everett L. Culver back to Templeton heirs in 1942, and was ultimately struck at a Sheriff's tax sale in October 1999 for $9,272.83.

The current mineral interest holder on record is Mary Lou Templeton Sanders, who has received royalty payments from operators GEO-VEST Incorporated, Titan Rock Exploration & Production, and most recently Sydri Operating, LLC (effective April 17, 2023) for wells in the Danville Field.

Section 02

Critical Findings

Critical: 1999 Sheriff's Tax Sale Bobby Weaver, Sheriff of Gregg County, conveyed ~173 acres (net ~119 acres) from Templeton et al to M M Investments via tax deed (Judgment: July 13, 1999; Sale: October 6, 1999) for $9,272.83. The deed conveys "all of the estate, right, title, and interest which the Defendants in such suit had" in the property, which includes mineral rights unless previously severed. The deed is subject to the owner's right of redemption under Section 34.21 of the Texas Property Tax Code. Whether the Templetons redeemed is the single most important unanswered question in this title.
High: Guardianship Deeds May Be Voidable The 1985-87 Warranty Deed (Vol. 1874, Pg. 551) was executed by Spiavia Templeton as Guardian of James Templeton (incompetent) and Vee McCalister as Guardian of Odie Templeton (NCM). No court orders authorizing these guardians to convey real property were found in this package. Without them, the conveyance is potentially voidable.
