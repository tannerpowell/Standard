Templeton Tract / Gregg County, TX

Net Mineral Acres: Research Playbook

A step-by-step plan to determine the complete mineral ownership on the ~119-173 acre Templeton tract, Haden Edwards Survey, Abstract 64. Work through these in order. Each step feeds the next.

Before Anything Else No public database gives you a net mineral acre breakdown for a specific tract. The NMA answer lives in two places: the operator's division orders (showing each owner's decimal interest) and the county deed records (showing the full chain of title). The steps below are the fastest path to both.

Phase 3

Professional Title Search

If the phone calls and database searches paint a picture worth pursuing, this is where you get the definitive answer. A landman pulls every recorded instrument in the chain and builds a runsheet showing exactly who owns what.

07
Hire a Gregg County Landman
$500 - $3,000 1-3 weeks
▶

What You Get

A mineral title runsheet: a spreadsheet listing every deed, mineral deed, royalty deed, assignment, probate, and tax sale in the chain. Each row shows document type, book/page, date, grantor, grantee, legal description, and the fractional interest conveyed or reserved. The bottom line is a net mineral acre calculation showing every current owner's share.

Why It's Non-Negotiable for This Tract

  • Multiple mineral severances (Hudson ~25%, Birdwell 10/83.6, Rio Grande Royalties unknown fraction)
  • A 1999 Sheriff's Tax Sale that may or may not have conveyed unsevered minerals
  • Guardianship deeds that may be voidable
  • A 1932 partition deed that's only partially in the package
  • Heirship questions for Luther Templeton, Charlie Templeton Sr., and others

No shortcut gets you past this complexity. You need someone sitting in the Gregg County Clerk's office pulling records.
