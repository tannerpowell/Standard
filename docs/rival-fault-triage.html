19 trucks have low engine coolant

Engine Coolant Level warnings are the most common critical fault in the Rival fleet. 19 vehicles are reporting low coolant, which risks overheating and catastrophic engine failure. In Permian Basin summer heat, a truck running low on coolant won't last. These need to be checked and topped off before they go out again.

ABS/VDC failures across the heavy fleet

Multiple trucks (Kenworths, Peterbilts, Internationals) are showing ABS wheel sensor and VDC system faults. These are Class 8 trucks running loaded in oilfield conditions. Compromised ABS means longer stopping distances at weight. 5 trucks have full ABS/VDC system communication failures.

Water in fuel on 8 trucks

The "Water In Fuel Indicator" code is showing up across 8 vehicles. Water in diesel causes injector damage, poor combustion, and accelerated fuel system wear. Common causes: contaminated fuel source, condensation in tanks (temperature swings), or failed water separator filters. If multiple trucks are fueling from the same source, that source needs to be tested.

PTO and transmission codes on 6 trucks

Enable Switch and Engagement Consent codes for the transmission input shaft PTO. These are oilfield service trucks that use PTO-driven equipment. If the PTO engagement is unreliable, that's a truck that can't do its job on-site.

Fleet-Wide Recommendations

Patterns in the diagnostic data that point to fleet-level actions.

Coolant System Sweep

19 trucks · All makes · Critical priority

Low engine coolant is the number one fault across the fleet. 19 trucks need coolant level checks and top-offs. In Permian Basin heat, a low-coolant truck is a blown head gasket waiting to happen. Check for leaks (radiator, hoses, water pump, EGR cooler) while topping off. If the same trucks keep coming back low, there's an underlying leak that needs repair.

Fuel Source Investigation

8 trucks · Water In Fuel · High priority

Eight trucks showing "Water In Fuel Indicator" codes. If they're fueling from the same source, that fuel supply needs to be tested immediately. Water in diesel destroys injectors ($500-2,000 each on a Class 8) and causes misfires and power loss. Drain water separators on all affected trucks, replace fuel filters, and verify the fuel source is clean.

ABS Sensor Inspection

11+ trucks · Wheel sensors · Critical priority

ABS wheel sensor codes on Axle 1 and Axle 2 across multiple trucks. These are heavy Class 8s (Kenworth T880, Peterbilt 367/389, International LT625) running loaded on rough oilfield roads. Bad wheel speed sensors mean the ABS can't do its job when a 80,000 lb truck needs to stop fast. Inspect sensor wiring, air gaps, and tone rings on all flagged units.

PTO System Service

6 trucks · Transmission PTO · High priority

PTO enable and engagement codes mean these trucks can't reliably power their on-site equipment. For an oilfield services fleet, a truck that can't engage its PTO is a truck that can't work. Check PTO wiring harnesses, engagement switches, and transmission PTO output shaft for wear.

Fuel-at-Fill-Up Logging

All 54 vehicles · Ongoing · High value

Rival's fuel analysis showed fleet-wide MPG of 3.70 from Samsara GPS data, but per-vehicle MPG was impossible because fuel cards don't map to individual trucks. Logging odometer and gallons at each fill-up would close that gap in 30 days. At $480K/year in fuel cost increase from the diesel spike, knowing which trucks are burning more than they should has real dollar value.
