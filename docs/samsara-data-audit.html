Naming Convention: TK- Is Everything

The TK- prefix covers both pickups (Ram 1500, F-150) and heavy-duty trucks (F-250, F-350, Ram 2500). Vehicle type has to be determined from make/model, not the unit prefix. For example, TK-357 is a Ram 1500 (pickup), TK-441 is an F-250 (3/4 ton), and EV.BD-065 is an International LT625 (Class 8). This matters for maintenance scheduling, since service intervals differ by vehicle class.

🔢 Odometer: Two Sources, One Winner

Samsara provides two odometer readings. Here's the difference and why it matters.

OBD Odometer Use This

Reads the vehicle's actual odometer through the OBD-II port. This is the real mileage, same as what the dashboard shows. 349 of 354 vehicles are reporting it.

GPS Odometer Supplemental

Counts distance traveled via GPS since the gateway was installed. Not the vehicle's true mileage, just miles-since-install. Only 81 vehicles have meaningful GPS odometer data.

Why this matters for maintenance

Service intervals are based on the vehicle's lifetime mileage (e.g., "oil change every 15,000 miles"). OBD odometer gives us that directly. GPS odometer would require knowing the install date and adding a baseline, which introduces error. We'll use OBD for all maintenance scheduling.

🔧 What This Unlocks for Maintenance Tracking

With this data already flowing, here's what a maintenance tracker app could do on day one

Service Interval Alerts (Mileage + Hours)

With live odometer and engine hour readings on 300+ vehicles, we can set service-due thresholds and get automatic alerts. No more checking spreadsheets or relying on memory. Oil change every 15,000 miles? The system tells you when TK-340 hits 700,000. PM every 500 engine hours? It flags EV.BD-068 when it crosses 34,000.

Fault Code Triage + AI Diagnosis

When fault code reporting is enabled, the API pushes J1939 codes (Class 8) and OBD-II codes (pickups) in real time. A maintenance app could catch those, run an AI diagnosis (root cause, estimated repair cost, in-house vs. outside shop recommendation), and create a work order automatically. Outside diagnosis runs $2,500 per event.

Coolant Temp + Oil Pressure Trending

The API already reports engine coolant temperature and oil pressure. Over time, we can build baseline profiles per vehicle and flag when readings drift outside normal range, catching overheating or lubrication issues before they become breakdowns. Preventive, not reactive.

Safety Event Integration

Hard braking, speeding, and collision events are coming through with driver names and dashcam video links. A maintenance tracker could correlate safety events with vehicle wear: a truck with frequent hard-braking events probably needs brake inspections more often. And the video evidence is right there for incident review.

GPS-Based Shop Routing

Every vehicle has a live GPS location with reverse geocoding (street address). When a truck needs service, the app can show where it is, what shop is closest, and whether it makes more sense to route to in-house or a nearby third party. Right now the fleet is clustered around Odessa, Midland, Pecos, Kermit, and Andrews.

Fuel Consumption + Cost Tracking

Fuel level %, odometer readings, and engine hours give us enough to calculate per-vehicle fuel burn for trucks over any time window. Combined with current diesel prices, that's a live fuel cost dashboard that replaces the manual Excel reports.

Equipment caveat: The AG51 trackers on heavy equipment (backhoes, dozers, etc.) only report GPS and battery. No engine hours, no fuel data. For the 50 pieces of equipment, fuel tracking will need manual logging of hours and fuel at the pump, or upgraded gateways that read engine data.

⛽ Recommendation: Per-Vehicle MPG Tracking

The single biggest data gap in the fleet right now, and a simple way to close it

The Problem

Right now, fuel cost estimates rely on assumed MPG by vehicle class: 11 MPG for a half-ton, 9.5 for a 3/4 ton, 3.7 for a Class 8. Those are reasonable averages, but averages hide a lot. A poorly-maintained truck running at 2.8 MPG costs 25% more in fuel than one running at 3.7. Across 354 vehicles and $2M+ in annual fuel spend, even a 10% variance is six figures.

Fuel cards can give us fleet-level MPG (total miles / total gallons), but the card names don't map 1:1 to unit numbers, so per-vehicle MPG is impossible from fuel card data alone.

The Fix: Odometer at Fill-Up

If drivers record their odometer reading and gallons pumped each time they refuel, we get real-world MPG for every vehicle, every fill-up. The math is simple: miles since last fill / gallons pumped = actual MPG.

Samsara already gives us the odometer reading in real time. But odometer at the pump, matched to gallons, closes the loop. A simple phone-friendly form at the fuel island, 30 seconds per fill-up, is all it takes.

What That Data Unlocks

Fuel cost accuracy Replace class-average MPG with real per-vehicle numbers. Fuel surcharge calculations become airtight and defensible to customers.
Maintenance signal A truck whose MPG drops 15% over a month has a problem: dirty air filter, injector issue, tire pressure, drag from a bad bearing. MPG degradation is often the first sign of a mechanical issue, before a fault code ever fires.
Driver behavior Two identical trucks on the same route shouldn't have wildly different MPG. If they do, it's a driver coaching opportunity (idling, speed, braking habits).
Fleet benchmarking Know which vehicles in the fleet are the most and least efficient. Inform disposal decisions: a 2017 International averaging 2.9 MPG might be costing more to operate than a replacement would cost to finance.
Audit trail GPS-verified odometer + fuel receipt = hard evidence. Useful for customer-facing surcharge justification, tax reporting, and catching fuel card misuse.

Implementation: Low Effort, High Payoff

This doesn't require new hardware. The Samsara gateways already report odometer. Drivers just need a quick way to log gallons at the pump. We can build a simple mobile form (scan or type unit number, enter gallons, confirm odometer) that writes to the same database the maintenance tracker uses. Once we have 30 days of fill-up data, we'll have real MPG baselines for every vehicle in the fleet.

At this fleet's scale (354 vehicles, $2M+/year in fuel), knowing actual per-vehicle efficiency pays for itself almost immediately.
