14 vehicles need immediate inspection

ABS and VDC (Vehicle Dynamic Control) system failures across multiple International belly dumps, plus low engine coolant warnings on 4 trucks and an accelerator pedal fault. These are braking safety and overheating risks. These units should not go to a jobsite until the codes are cleared.

Cylinder misfires are epidemic in the pickup fleet

38+ vehicles (mostly Ram 2500s and Ford F-150s) are throwing misfire codes across multiple cylinders. This is the single most common fault in the fleet. Misfires cause rough running, reduced power, worse fuel economy, and accelerated catalytic converter damage. The pattern suggests a systemic issue: deferred spark plug or ignition coil maintenance across a wave of trucks that all hit service age around the same time.

Turbo underboost on 20+ Ram 2500s

Turbocharger underboost and cold-start boost control codes showing up across the Ram 2500 fleet. These trucks are losing power under load. In oilfield conditions (towing, hills, heat), that's both a productivity and a safety issue. Common causes: wastegate actuator, boost pressure leaks, or intercooler failures.

DEF fluid levels are low across 15+ trucks

Multiple vehicles showing "Reductant Level Too Low." This is the easiest fix on the list: top off the DEF tanks. But if ignored, the emissions system will eventually force an engine derate (reduced power), which can sideline a truck mid-job. A DEF top-off sweep across the fleet would clear these in a day.

350 codes excluded (Sold & Out of Service)

This report only covers vehicles marked Active or In Shop. 37 vehicles marked Sold or Out of Service had 350 additional fault codes that are excluded since they don't need action. If any of those trucks are still physically on-premises, their codes may be worth reviewing before auction or disposal.

Fleet-Wide Recommendations

Patterns in the diagnostic data that suggest fleet-level actions rather than one-off repairs.

Spark Plug & Ignition Coil Campaign

38+ pickups · Ram 2500, F-150 · High priority

The misfire epidemic across the pickup fleet points to aging ignition components. A bulk campaign would be far more efficient than chasing individual trucks. Order coils and plugs in bulk for the affected models, schedule a rotation through the shop over 2-3 weeks.

ABS Sensor Sweep on Belly Dumps

10+ International EV.BD units · Critical priority

Nearly all ABS/VDC faults are on International belly dumps used in off-road environmental work. Wheel speed sensor wiring takes a beating on rough terrain. A dedicated harness and sensor inspection across the EV.BD fleet could clear these in a few days and restore braking safety.

DEF Fluid Top-Off Sweep

15+ trucks · All divisions · Same-day fix

Multiple trucks are running low on diesel exhaust fluid. This is the lowest-effort, highest-return fix on the list. One person with a DEF tank can clear these codes in a single day. Ignoring them leads to engine derate, which means a truck pulling to the side of the road mid-job because the ECU won't let it exceed 5 mph.

Turbo Inspection on Ram 2500 Fleet

20+ Ram 2500s · Environmental, Restraints · High priority

Underboost codes on this many trucks of the same make suggest a common failure mode. Check wastegate actuators, boost pressure plumbing, and intercooler connections. These trucks are losing power under load, which in towing conditions means harder braking, more fuel burn, and longer job times.

Fuel-at-Fill-Up Logging Program

All 308 active vehicles · Ongoing · High value

Drivers record odometer and gallons each time they refuel. Simple phone form, 30 seconds at the pump. After 30 days, you have real per-vehicle MPG baselines instead of class-average estimates. At $2M+/year in fuel spend, knowing which trucks are burning 25% more than they should pays for itself almost immediately. MPG degradation is also an early signal for mechanical issues (dirty filters, injector problems, tire pressure) before a fault code ever fires.

Preventive Maintenance Scheduling

All vehicles · Ongoing · Infrastructure

Samsara is reporting live odometer on 349 vehicles and engine hours on 311. This data can drive automatic PM scheduling: oil changes at mileage intervals, filter replacements at hour intervals, brake inspections at safety-event thresholds. Right now, 51% of the active fleet has at least one fault code. A PM program that catches problems before they throw codes would bring that number down significantly and reduce unplanned downtime.

Fault Code Alerting Workflow

Infrastructure · Immediate

Meghan turned on fault code alerts and got 100+ notifications in 3 minutes. That's a firehose with no filter. The next step is a triage workflow: codes hit an app (not an inbox), get auto-classified by severity, and route to the right person. Critical codes trigger an SMS. High codes create a work order. Medium/low queue up for the next scheduled PM. Nobody should be manually sorting 100 push notifications.

Equipment Hour Tracking

50 pieces heavy equipment · Ongoing

The AG51 trackers on backhoes, dozers, and excavators only report GPS location and battery. No engine hours, no fuel data. For equipment that costs $20-40/hour in fuel alone, this is a data gap. Either upgrade to full engine-reading gateways, or (cheaper) have operators log hours at shift end. Paired with the fuel-at-fill-up program, this gives real GPH data for the equipment fleet instead of flat 7hrs/day assumptions.

The bigger picture

This fleet grew fast. 354 tracked vehicles, 308 active, across multiple divisions and geographies. The data infrastructure is excellent (Samsara gateways on nearly everything, 95% dashcam coverage, 99% OBD odometer). What's missing is the layer between raw telematics and operational decisions. Right now, a fault code fires and becomes one of 100 push notifications. A truck burns 25% more fuel than it should and nobody knows until someone rebuilds a spreadsheet.

The hardware is already doing its job. The gap is software that turns that data into a maintenance queue, a fuel cost dashboard, and service schedules that run themselves. Everything in this report was pulled from the API in under a minute. The data is there. It just needs a home.
